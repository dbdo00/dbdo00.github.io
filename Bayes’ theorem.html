Bayes’ theorem

Bayes’s theorem is still not very intuitive to me. So I just want to write about it.

Conditional probability

The probability of an event BB given an event AA is denoted by P(A|B)P(A | B ). Formally, it is defined as P(A∩B)P(B)\frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}.

This value is named conditional probability.

Inference

If you know P(B|A)P(B|A), i.e. the conditional probability of BB given AA, and you want to know P(A|B)P(A|B), then you are trying to do an inference: by the result, determining the reason.The other two information you need in order to infer are P(A)P(A) and P(B)P(B), as in the Bayes’s theorem:

P(A|B)=(P(B|A)P(A))/(P(B)) P(A|B) = (P(B|A) P(A))/(P(B))
