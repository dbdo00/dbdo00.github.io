Invariant Space

id:: 6932e5e1-39b7-450d-b428-3f1afad9e70e
- Every invariant Space of $T\in \mathcal L(T)$ is a direct sum of spaces spanned by eigenvectors if $T$ is diagonalizable. Suppose $V$ is an invariant space of $T$.
- The idea is to show $V=\oplus (V\cap V_\lambda)$
- Of course, $\subset$ is obvious.
- On the other hand, if $v = v_1 + \ldots + v_n$, where $v_i$ are eigenvectors with different eigenvalues, then we need to show $v_i \in V$ for each $i$. Use lagrange interpolation to construct a polynomial. $f(\lambda_k)=1$ if $k=i$ and $0$ otherwise. Thus $f(T)v_k = f(\lambda_k)v_k$. This gives $f(A)v=v_i$. So $v_i \in V$ by the invariance of $V$.
- For a general $T\colon: V\to V$, an invariant space is a direct sum of spaces spanned by general eigenvectors. I do not know how this is done exactly.q

  • Lower Bound of Unipotent Matrix

    • For $A$ a matrix, if $A-I$ is nilpotent, then $A$ is said to be unipotent.
    • Define $|A| = \max |a_{ij}|$. Then $|A|$ is lower bounded by $1$. Surprisingly, this can be proved using trace. In fact, there is at least one diagonal entry whose absolute value is not less than $1$.
    • A thing I didn't grasp is that the trace is equal to the sum of eigenvalues counting the multiplicity. For the power, the trace is the sum of eigenvalues taken the power, counting the multiplicity.
    • $$\mathrm{trace} A^n= \sum \lambda_i^n$$
    • This is clear from the Jordan canonical form and I do not know how can one show this without JCF.
    • The upper bound of $|A^j|$ is $Kj^m$, where $K$ is a positive constant.

  • Gluing Lemma

    • For $X\subset \mathbb R^n$, if $X=A\cap B$ and $A,B$ are closed sets, and $f\mid A$, $f\mid B$ are both continuous, then $f$ is continuous.
    • I saw that this condition can be weakened to Hausdorff space, but I didn't look into it.
    • This lemma is useful for algebraic topology.
