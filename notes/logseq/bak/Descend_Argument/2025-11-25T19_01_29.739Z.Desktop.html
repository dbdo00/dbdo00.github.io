This notes discusses the proof of Fermat's theorem about sum of squares, which is described as a "descend argument".

Fermat propose the following proposition

A prime number $p$ is a sum of two squares $a^2 + b^2$, where $a$ and $b$
are coprime, if and only if $p \equiv 1 \pmod 4$.

  • An integer $a$ is congruent to another integer $a^\prime$ such that $|a^\prime|<p/2$.

  • Let $N=a^2 +b^2$, $\gcd(a,b)=1$. If all primes divisors of $N$ are sum of squares, then $p>0$, the greatest primes divisor of $N$, is a sum of squares.

The contrapositive is that: if $p$ is not a sum of squares, then there exists
some $q$, $q\mid N$ and $q<p$, such that $q$ is not a sum of squares.

Then $q | N$ and is not a sum of squares. We can find a prime divisor smaller than $q$ that is not a sum of squares... So we get a decreasing sequence of prime numbers. A contradiction is found.
