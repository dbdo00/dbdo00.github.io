Probability

Theorem 1 (Markov's inequality)
$P(X\geq \lambda) \leq \frac{E(X)}{\lambda}$

Proof.
$\int^\infty_0 xf_X(x)dx \geq \int^\infty_\lambda \lambda f_X(x)dx$
since $x f_X(x) > \lambda f_X(x)$ for $x >\lambda$.

The proof for the discrete case is similar and so omitted.

Lemma 1.2 (Borel-Cantelli lemma) Let $E_1, E_2 \ldots$ be a sequence of events (possibly infinite or dependent), such that $\sum_n P(E_n) < \infty$. Then for any integer $M$, we have

$$P(\text{fewer than $M$ of the events} E_1, E_2, \ldots, \text{ hold}) \geq 1 - \frac{\sum_n P(E_n)}{M}$$

Proof. The value of $\sum_n I(E_n)$ is the number events in $E_1, E_2, \ldots$ that hold. $E(\sum_n I(E)) = \sum_n P(E_n)$. The result follows from markov inequality with $\lambda = M$.

Sum-Free

The random variable $k$ and the value $a$ is assumed to be independent. It does not matter what the probability distribution is — any distribution works.
