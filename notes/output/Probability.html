Conditional Probability of a Uniform distribution

In a finite probability space with uniform distribution, the probability of a set is just its size divided by the total set. This leads to the equation that $P(A|B)= \frac{|A\cap B|}{|B|}$. In addition, the fact that every singleton has equal probabilities results in that $P(x|B) = \frac{1}{|B|}$ for all $x\in B$.

In general, the conditional probability conditioned on the event $A$ is a "subprobability", that is defined on the subspace (of the event space $\Sigma$)
$$\mathcal T = { S \in \Sigma | S \subset A}.$$
It suffices to define the probability conditioned on $A$ of each singleton in a finite probability space. The space $\mathcal T$ has the probability measure $P_{\mathcal T}(S)=\frac{|S|}{|A|}$ which is equal to the conditional probability of $S$ conditioned on $A$. Note how the uniform probability measure is inherited to the subspace via defining the conditional probability as the "sub-measure".
