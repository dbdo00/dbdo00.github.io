Exercise 1

[2 points]. Prove that the eigenvalues of a real symmetric matrix $A \in R^{N ×N}$ ,
where $A = A^\top$ , are real-valued (i.e., not complex-valued). Prove also that
the eigenvectors of A corresponding to different eigenvalues are orthogonal
to each other

Proof.

For $v,\ w \in \mathbb C^N$, let $\langle v, w \rangle$ denote $v^\top \bar w$ , where $\bar w$ is the complex conjugate of the vector $w$.

Let $t \in \mathbb C$. Then $$\begin{equation}
\bar t \langle v,\ w\rangle = \bar t v^\top \bar w = v^\top \bar t \bar w = v^\top \overline{t w} = \langle v,\ tw\rangle \end{equation}. $$
$$\begin{equation} t\langle v,\ w \rangle = tv^\top \bar w= (tv)^\top \overline w = \langle tv,\ w \rangle \end{equation}
$$
$$\begin{equation} \langle v,\ v \rangle = v^\top \bar v= v_1 \overline v_1 + \ldots + v_N \overline v_N \geq 0\end{equation} $$
where $v = (v_1, \ \ldots ,\ v_N)^\top$.
Let $A^$ denote the complex transpose of a matrix $A$. Then $$\langle v, Av \rangle = v^\top \overline{Av}=v^\top \overline{A^\top}^\top v=v^\top (A^)^\top v = (A^* v)^\top v = \langle A^* v ,\ v \rangle$$ Suppose $v$ is an eigenvector of $A$ associated with the eigenvalue $\lambda$.

If $\langle v,\ v\rangle = 0$, then $v_1 \overline v_1 + \ldots + v_N \overline v_N =0$. Since $v_i \overline v_i \geq 0$ and $=0$ only when $v_i=0$, we know $$v_i=0 \quad {\rm for \ each\ } i \in {1,\ldots,N}.$$Hence $v=0$. In this case, the eigenvalue of $v$ is $0$, which is real valued.

If $\langle v,\ v\rangle >0$, then
$$\overline\lambda = \frac{1}{\langle v,\ v\rangle}\overline \lambda \langle v,\ v\rangle $$
By Eq $\boxed{\overline t\langle v,\ w \rangle=\langle v,\ tw \rangle}$, we get
$$\overline \lambda =\frac{1}{\langle v,\ v\rangle} \langle v,\ \lambda v\rangle =\frac{1}{\langle v,\ v\rangle} \langle v,\ Av \rangle$$ By $\boxed {\langle v, Av \rangle = \langle A^* v ,\ v \rangle}$, we get
$$\overline \lambda = \frac{1}{\langle v,\ v\rangle} \langle A^* v ,\ v \rangle=\frac{1}{\langle v,\ v\rangle} \langle \lambda v,\ v\rangle = \frac{1}{\langle v,\ v\rangle} \lambda \langle v,\ v\rangle = \lambda$$
So we have derived that $\boxed{\overline \lambda = \lambda}$. So any eigenvalue of $A$ is real-valued.

Suppose $v,\ w$ are eigenvectors of $A$ associated with $\lambda_v$ and $\lambda_w$. Then $$\langle Av,\ w \rangle = \lambda_v \langle v,\ w \rangle$$ and since $A\in \mathbb R^{N\times N}$, $$\langle Av,\ w \rangle = \langle v,\ A^*w \rangle = \langle v,\ A^\top w \rangle=\langle v,\ Aw \rangle = \lambda_w \langle v,\ w \rangle.$$
So $$0 =\langle Av,\ w \rangle - \langle Av,\ w \rangle = (\lambda_v - \lambda_w) \langle v,\ w \rangle$$ which implies $\langle v,\ w \rangle = 0$ because $\lambda_v - \lambda_w \neq 0$.
