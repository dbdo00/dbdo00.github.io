Separability

  1. If $F$ is a perfect field with characteristic $p>0$, then every element
    has a $p$-th root.

Proof. Let $a$ be an arbitary element in $F$.
Let $\alpha$ be a root of the polynomial $x^p -a$.
Since the field has characteristic $p$,
$x^p - a = x^p - \alpha^p = (x-\alpha)^p$.
So the polynomial has repeated roots, hence not irreducible in $F$,
because $F$ is a perfect field in which every irreducible polynomial
is separable, i.e. has no repeated roots. So, the minimal polynomial
$f(x)$ of $\alpha$ over $F$ has degree $d< p$.
Since $f(x)\mid (x-\alpha)^p$, we conclude that $f(x)=(x-\alpha)^d =x^d + \ldots + (-\alpha)^d$ is the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$, and so $\alpha^d \in F$.
Suppose $ap + bd =1$. Then $\alpha = \alpha^{ap+bd} =(\alpha^p)^a (\alpha^d)^b \in F$. So, the $p$-th root of $a$ is in $F$. $\square$

Remark: I believe that the above argument also proves the following: Over a field $K$ (of any char), $x^p -a$ is either irreducible or has completely reducible (i.e. has the root in K).

The converse proposition is also true:

  1. In a field $F$ with characteristic $p>0$, if every element has a $p$-th root, then $F$ is a perfect field.

Proof. Let $f(x)\in F[x]$ be irreducible.
Suppose $f(x)$ has repeated roots, then $f^\prime(x)=0$.
But this means that $f(x)=[g(x)]^p$ for some $g(x)\in F[x]$. Suppose

$$g(x)=a_nx^n + \ldots a_1x + a_0.$$

Then $f(x)= (g(x))^p = (a_nx^n + \ldots a_1x^n + a_0)^p = a_n^px^{pn} + \ldots + a_1^px^{p} + a_0^p$. Since $a_k^{p}\in F$, $a_k\in F$. So $g(x)\in F[x]$.
This contradicts that $f(x)$ is irreducible. Q.E.D

Exercises

  1. Give an example where $Gal(E/F)/Gal(E/B) \cong G$ where $G$ is a proper subgroup of Gal(B/F).

  2. Find the fixed field of the subgroup $\{ \alpha_1, \alpha_4\}$, where $\alpha_1(\xi) = \xi, \alpha_4(\xi)=\xi^4$. Check that Theorem that $[K:K^G] =|G|$.

Solution. Note that

$$(x- (\xi + \xi^4))(x - (\xi^3 + \xi^2)) = x^2 + x + 1$$

from which we know that $[\mathbb Q (\xi + \xi^4): \mathbb Q ] =2$.
Since $[K^G: \mathbb Q] =2$ and $K^G \supset \mathbb Q (\xi + \xi^4)$,
we know that $K^G = \mathbb Q (\xi + \xi^4)$, from which $[K:K^G]=2=G$. $\square$

  1. The Galois group of an irreducible polynomial $f$ is transitive on the set of zeros of $f$.

Solution. If $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are two zeros of $f$, then their minimal polynomials are equal. By theorem, there exists $\gamma$ in the Galois group of $f$ such that $\gamma (\alpha) = \beta$. $\square$
