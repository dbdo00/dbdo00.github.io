  • 8.1.2 Prove that the real projective space $P^n$ is a manifold by constructing, for any $0\leq i\leq n$, a homeomorphism between the subset
    $$U_i = \{\text{span}\{(x_0,x_1, \ldots, x_n )\} \colon x_i \neq 0 \} \subset P^n$$
    and $\mathbb R^n$. Similarly, prove that the complex projective space is also a manifold.

Relevant Definitions. We denote space spanned by the vector $(x_1,\dots, x_n)$ with thenotation $[x_1: \dots : x_n]$. This definition implies that $[\lambda x_1 : \dots : \lambda x_n] = [x_1 : \dots : x_n]$. The real linear projective space $P^n$ is the set of $1$-dimensional linear subspaces of $\mathbb R^n$. Let $\phi\colon \mathbb R^n \to P^n$, $(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\mapsto [x_1:\dots:x_n]$. A set $V \in P^n$ is open if and only $\phi^{-1}(V)$ is open, by the definition of the topology of $P^n$.

Answer.
We will use the universal properties of the subspace and quotient topology.
In this proof, there is not much geometries going on; instead everything is just
the application of the universal properties.

Let $S=:\mathbb R^n \setminus \{0\} - \{(v_1,\ldots,v_n)\in \mathbb R^n | v_i = 0\}$.
We endow $S$ with the subspace topology of $\mathbb R^n$. Also, the quotient map restricted on $S$ has the image $U_i$. Hence $\pi\colon : S \to U_i$ is well defined.
Since $f\colon S\to \mathbb R^n$, $(v_1, \ldots, v_n) \to (\frac{v_1}{v_i}, \ldots, \frac{v_n}{v_i})$ is a map such that $f(x) = f(y) \iff \exists \lambda \in \mathbb R \,s.t. \, x=\lambda y \iff \pi(x)=\pi(y)$, there exists a unique map $g\colon U_i \to \mathbb R^{n-1}$ such that $f=g\circ \pi$. This map $f$ is given by $[v_1: \dots : v_i : \dots : v_n] \mapsto (v_1, \ldots , v_n)$.

Now, $g$ is continuous if and only if $f$ is continuous. Is $f$ continuous? The inclusion
map $\iota_S: S\to \mathbb R^n$ is certainly continuous. Also, the inclusion map $\iota_{\mathbb{R}^{n-1}} \to \mathbb R^n$ is continuous. Since $\iota_S = \iota_{\mathbb R^{n-1}} \circ f$, we know that $f$ is continuous, by the universal property of the subspace $\mathbb R^{n-1} \subset \mathbb R^n$. So, it follows that $g$ is continuous.

We need to show that the reverse of $g$ is continuous as well. It decomposes to a composition of continuous maps:

$$\mathbb R^{n-1} \quad \overset{(v_1, \ldots, v_n) \mapsto (v_1, \ldots, 1, \ldots ,v_n)}{\to} \quad S \overset{\pi}{\to} U_i$$
.
