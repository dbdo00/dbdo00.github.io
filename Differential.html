Differential

(Just a note on the differential of a function ℝm→ℝn\mathbb{R}^m \to \mathbb{R}^n) The differntial (at a point) is defined as an mm to nn matrix in the very beginning. Notationally, we refer to the differential function of f:ℝm→ℝnf\colon \mathbb{R}^m \to \mathbb{R}^n by DfDf.

Since differential is a linear approximation, at least the dimensions need to match. That is why DfDf is a linear function ℝm→ℝn\mathbb{R}^m \to \mathbb{R}^n. In particular, it is an mm by nn matrix

Each row of Df(x)Df(x) is the differential of fi(x)f_i(x), the ii-th component of ff.

Munkres uses one of a little weird notation for the directional derivative at the point uu, that is f′(a;u)f^\prime(a;u). It makes sense though. I do find it likable.

From the definition of differential, we can derive Theorem 5.1 in Munkres’s book

f′(a;u)=Df(a)⋅u. f'(a; u) = Df(a) \cdot u .

This in turn gives Theorem 5.3
 Df(a)=[D1f(a)D2f(a)…Dm(f)].Df(a) = \begin{bmatrix} D_1 f(a) & D_2 f(a) & \ldots & D_m(f) \end{bmatrix}.

This is the simple case of A→ℝ1A \to \mathbb{R}^1. For the more general case of A→ℝnA \to \mathbb{R}^n, just evaluate the limit

F(h)=f(a+h)−f(a)−B⋅h|h| F(h) = \frac{ f(a+h) - f( a) - B \cdot h}{| h|}

by row.
