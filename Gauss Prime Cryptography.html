Gauss Prime Cryptography

Gauss Integer



2025-09-21 The Gauss integers are complex numbers with integer real and imarginary parts. Let z=a+biz=a+bi where a,ba,b are integers. Then we define N(z)=a2+b2N(z)=a^2 +b^2

Visually, Gauss integers are integer grids on the complex plane. A complex number always falls in a grid, on the non-vertex edges, or are the vertices. Suppose z=a+biz=a+bi is a complex number.

Question Find a Gauss integer z′z' as close to zz as possible.

We round a,ba,b both to the elosest integers a′,b′a', b' so that |a′−a|≤1/2|b′−b|≤1/2.\begin{align*} |a' - a | \leq 1/2 \\ |b' - b| \leq 1/2 \end{align*}. from which |z−(a′+b′i)|≤1/2|z - (a' + b'i) | \leq 1/2
So setting z′=a+biz'=a+bi gives an answer.

If we loose the condition and only require the unit disk (excluding the boundry ) around the Gauss integer z′z' to cover zz, then some other vertices would also suffice as the choices of z′z'. But anyway, we have shown that every complex number is inside the unit disk around some Gauss prime on the complex plane.
